# GEOG5013M
The UK Government’s commitment to delivering 1.5 million homes in England between 2024 and 2029 raises important questions regarding the long-term energy implications of future housing development. This study quantifies the potential impact of alternative building standards on residential space heating demand across English Local Authority Districts. Over 680,000 Energy Performance Certificate records for dwellings constructed between 2022 and 2026 were analysed to establish archetypal dwelling characteristics, including floor area, heating systems and estimated space-heating demand. These archetypes were combined with Local Housing Need numbers to model and compare future heating demand under three scenarios: continuation of current Part L building regulations, the proposed Future Homes Standard (FHS), and the Passivhaus standard. 

This repo contains the code and files nessesary to recreate the projections of this initial investigation. Including functions that:
- Baseline properties of new build dwellings post 2021 Part L uplift. Using bulk EPC records.
- Generate estimations of future heat demand and English Local Authority level for current Part L building regulations, the proposed Future    Homes Standard (FHS), and the Passivhaus standard.
- Convert Heat Demand estimations to estimations of fuel (gas) and electricity demand.
- Convert fuel (gas) and electricity demand values to running cost estimations.
- Map results and carry out intial exploratory analysis on results
